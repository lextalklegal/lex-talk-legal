DRAT Appeal From DRT Order: Limitation, Pre-Deposit and Appeal Process Explained

If a party is dissatisfied with a DRT order, one of the most important questions is whether an appeal lies to DRAT, within what time, and what pre-deposit is required. This guide explains the statutory framework under the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993.

What is DRAT?

DRAT stands for Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunal. The RDB Act establishes an Appellate Tribunal framework for appeals against orders of the DRT.

How much time is available to appeal a DRT order?

Section 20(3) of the RDB Act provides that an appeal under that section shall be filed within 30 days from the date on which a copy of the order made, or deemed to have been made, by the Tribunal is received by the person filing the appeal.

Because limitation depends on the statutory language and the facts of service/receipt, litigants should calculate the period from the actual order and record rather than relying on an informal date.

Is there a pre-deposit for a borrower?

Yes. Section 21 of the RDB Act provides that where an appeal is preferred by a person from whom the debt is due to a bank or financial institution, the appeal shall not be entertained unless 50% of the amount of debt due as determined by the Tribunal under section 19 is deposited with the Appellate Tribunal.

The statute also permits the Appellate Tribunal, for reasons recorded in writing, to reduce the amount to be deposited to not less than 25% of the debt referred to in the provision.

Does this pre-deposit apply in exactly the same way to every appeal?

No. Section 21 is specific to appeals under the RDB Act in the circumstances stated there. Other statutes can contain different appeal provisions. For example, the SARFAESI Act has its own section 18 framework for appeals against DRT orders under section 17, including its own pre-deposit language.

What documents are normally important for a DRAT appeal?

The complete DRT order is central. Other important material can include the pleadings, written statement, evidence, interim orders, applications, bank documents, account statements, SARFAESI notices where relevant, and the procedural history.

Can a party ask for interim protection?

Parties may seek interim relief in appropriate proceedings, but whether protection is granted depends on the statutory powers, pleadings, record and order of the appellate forum. An appeal should not be treated as an automatic stay of a DRT order unless an actual order granting protection says so.

Common DRAT mistakes

  • Calculating limitation from memory rather than the actual receipt date.
  • Filing without checking which statute creates the appeal.
  • Ignoring the statutory pre-deposit requirement.
  • Submitting an appeal without the complete DRT record.
  • Assuming that filing an appeal automatically stops recovery.

Practical takeaway

Before filing a DRT appeal, identify the governing statute first. Under the RDB Act, section 20 provides the appeal and section 21 provides the pre-deposit rule. Under SARFAESI, section 18 provides the appeal against an order under section 17 and contains a similar but separately worded pre-deposit regime.

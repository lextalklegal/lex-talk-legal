SARFAESI Section 13(4) Explained: Possession, Sale of Secured Assets and DRT Remedy

What does SARFAESI Section 13(4) mean? What is symbolic possession? When can a secured creditor sell the secured asset? What remedy does a borrower have? This guide breaks down the statutory framework.

Statutory provisions, rules, notifications, judicial decisions and facts of individual matters can change or interact differently depending on the case. What is Section 13(4) of SARFAESI?

Section 13(4) of the SARFAESI Act deals with specified measures that a secured creditor may take when the borrower has not discharged the secured liability within the period stated in the demand notice and the statutory conditions for enforcement are met.

The official India Code text identifies several measures. Among them is taking possession of the secured assets of the borrower, including the right to transfer by lease, assignment or sale for realising the secured asset. The statutory text also provides other measures in specified circumstances.

What are the main measures under Section 13(4)?

In simplified terms, the measures in section 13(4) include:

  • taking possession of the secured assets;
  • taking over management of the business of the borrower in the situations specified by the Act;
  • appointing a manager to manage the secured assets; and
  • requiring certain persons who acquired the secured asset from the borrower not to deal with it except with the secured creditor's prior written consent.

The exact measure used depends on the security, the account, the statutory conditions and the enforcement strategy adopted by the secured creditor.

What is symbolic possession?

“Symbolic possession” is a commonly used practical expression in SARFAESI proceedings. It is generally associated with taking possession through the statutory enforcement process without the secured creditor necessarily obtaining immediate physical control of the property.

The precise legal effect of a possession notice depends on the applicable statutory provisions, security interest, possession procedure and facts. It should not automatically be assumed that every possession notice has identical legal consequences.

What is physical possession?

Physical possession means the secured creditor or its authorised representative obtains actual possession/control of the secured asset in accordance with the applicable law and procedure. Where assistance is required for taking possession, section 14 of SARFAESI provides a statutory mechanism involving the Chief Metropolitan Magistrate or District Magistrate in the circumstances specified by the Act.

Can the property be sold after possession?

Yes, SARFAESI's enforcement framework contemplates transfer of the secured asset by lease, assignment or sale for realising the secured asset. The sale process is also governed by the applicable rules, including the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, and by the facts of the case.

Accordingly, a borrower should not treat the word “possession” as meaning that the sale has already occurred. Possession and sale are separate enforcement stages.

Can a borrower challenge Section 13(4) action?

Section 17 of SARFAESI provides a remedy before the DRT to a person, including a borrower, aggrieved by a measure referred to in section 13(4). The current statutory text provides for an application to the DRT within 45 days from the date on which the measure was taken, subject to the statutory framework and jurisdictional provisions.

The DRT considers whether the measures taken by the secured creditor comply with the Act and the rules. If the Tribunal finds the measures invalid, the Act gives it powers including declaring the recourse invalid and, where appropriate, restoring possession or management to the borrower or other aggrieved person.

Which DRT has jurisdiction?

Section 17(1A) identifies jurisdictional connecting factors, including where the cause of action wholly or partly arises, where the secured asset is located, or where the relevant bank account is maintained, subject to the detailed statutory wording.

Does Section 17 cover a mere 13(2) notice?

Section 17 is expressly tied to measures referred to in section 13(4). The statutory text separately states that communication of the reasons for not accepting a representation or objection at the 13(2) stage does not itself entitle a person to make an application under section 17 merely because of that communication.

Section 13(4) versus Section 14

ProvisionRole
Section 13(4)Specifies enforcement measures available to the secured creditor in the statutory circumstances.
Section 14Provides a mechanism for assistance in taking possession through the CMM/DM in the situations covered by the Act.
Section 17Provides the DRT remedy against measures referred to in section 13(4).

Practical checklist when possession action starts

  • Keep the demand notice and all subsequent notices in one file.
  • Record dates carefully; limitation periods can depend on the specific measure and governing provision.
  • Check the property description against title and security documents.
  • Preserve payment records and correspondence with the lender.
  • Check whether the enforcement procedure and applicable rules were followed.
  • Obtain case-specific legal advice where possession, sale or third-party rights are involved.

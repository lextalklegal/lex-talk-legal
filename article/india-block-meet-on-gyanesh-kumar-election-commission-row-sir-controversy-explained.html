INDIA Block Meet on Gyanesh Kumar: Election Commission Row, SIR Controversy Explained

By Lex Talk Legal Editorial Desk · Educational & informational coverage · Original source

INDIA Block Meet on Gyanesh Kumar: Election Commission Row, SIR Controversy and What Happened

New Delhi | September 30, 2026



The ongoing dispute surrounding the Election Commission of India (ECI), Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls has become a major political issue.

On September 30, leaders of several opposition parties belonging to the INDIA bloc are meeting in New Delhi to discuss their response to the controversy and formulate a joint strategy. Reports indicate that the meeting is expected to focus on electoral-roll revisions, concerns raised about the functioning of the poll panel and possible parliamentary action concerning the Chief Election Commissioner.

At the same time, government and Election Commission sources have rejected the Opposition's criticism of the poll panel and defended the SIR exercise, saying that the revision process is legally backed and intended to identify ineligible or duplicate entries while protecting the rights of eligible voters.

Why is the INDIA bloc meeting?

The meeting comes amid an escalating political dispute over the functioning of the Election Commission and the revision of electoral rolls.

According to reports, more than 20 political parties and their representatives are expected to participate in the discussions. The INDIA bloc is considering a coordinated response, including possible nationwide protests and parliamentary action related to the CEC.

The issue gained further momentum after reports emerged about disagreements within the three-member Election Commission regarding decisions connected with the SIR process.

What is the controversy involving Gyanesh Kumar?

The controversy intensified after an Indian Express investigation reported that Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi had raised objections on several occasions concerning decisions and orders associated with electoral-roll revisions.

The report said that objections had been recorded multiple times over a period of around 10 months. Opposition parties subsequently cited these reports while questioning the manner in which decisions were being taken within the Commission.

However, the Election Commission has presented a different account.

Following a meeting of the full three-member Commission on September 26, the ECI said that important decisions concerning SIR had been taken unanimously. The Commission also maintained that differing views during institutional deliberations are a normal part of decision-making.

Therefore, the reported internal objections and the Election Commission's response remain two important sides of the current dispute.

What is SIR?

SIR stands for Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls.

The exercise is aimed at reviewing voter lists and identifying issues such as deceased voters, duplicate entries and voters who may have shifted from a particular area.

The Election Commission has said that the process is intended to improve the accuracy of electoral rolls while ensuring that eligible voters are not excluded. In its latest clarification, the ECI also said that Booth Level Officers would visit the homes of certain voters who receive notices to collect required documents.

The Opposition, however, has raised concerns about the possibility of genuine voters being excluded from electoral rolls and has demanded greater transparency concerning additions, deletions and corrections.

What is the Opposition demanding?

Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge, while addressing the Congress Working Committee on September 29, demanded the removal of CEC Gyanesh Kumar and raised concerns about the transparency of electoral-roll changes.

Among the demands raised by the Congress were disclosure of data relating to significant changes in voter lists, information and appeal opportunities for voters whose names have been removed, and greater transparency in the electoral process.

The INDIA bloc meeting is expected to consider a broader joint strategy on these issues.

Some reports also indicate that opposition parties are considering a fresh parliamentary removal motion concerning the Chief Election Commissioner.

Election Commission's response

The Election Commission has rejected the suggestion that internal differences indicate that the institution is being controlled by one individual.

The Commission has stated that observations and differences during deliberations are part of the institutional process and that decisions relating to SIR were taken by the full Commission.

The ECI has also defended the legality of the electoral-roll revision exercise and said that its objective is to maintain accurate voter lists.

This position has also been supported publicly by BJP leaders, who have criticised the Opposition's allegations against the Election Commission.

Why the issue matters

The dispute is significant because electoral rolls form a fundamental part of the election process.

The central questions being debated include:

  • How are additions and deletions from electoral rolls verified?

  • What safeguards are available to voters whose names are removed?

  • How transparent should electoral-roll changes be?

  • How are disagreements between Election Commissioners handled?

  • What parliamentary and legal mechanisms are available concerning the removal of a Chief Election Commissioner?

These questions are now being discussed politically as well as through institutional and legal processes.

What happens next?

The immediate focus is on the outcome of the INDIA bloc meeting and whether opposition parties agree on a common course of action.

The Election Commission, meanwhile, is continuing the SIR process and has reiterated its position that the exercise is intended to improve the accuracy of electoral rolls.

With Parliament, political parties, the Election Commission and courts potentially involved in different aspects of the dispute, the controversy surrounding Gyanesh Kumar and the electoral-roll revision process is likely to remain an important issue in the coming weeks.

Key Takeaways

Gyanesh Kumar: Chief Election Commissioner of India and central figure in the current controversy.

INDIA bloc meeting: Opposition parties are meeting in New Delhi on September 30 to discuss their response to the Election Commission-related controversy.

SIR: Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls, aimed at reviewing and updating voter lists.

Opposition's position: Opposition parties have raised concerns about transparency, electoral-roll changes and the functioning of the Election Commission.

ECI's position: The Commission says SIR is a lawful electoral-roll revision exercise and that important decisions were taken unanimously.

Next step: The INDIA bloc is expected to discuss coordinated political and parliamentary action.

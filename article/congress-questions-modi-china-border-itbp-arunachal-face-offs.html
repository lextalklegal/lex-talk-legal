Congress Questions Modi Government on China Border After ITBP Chief Reports Arunachal Face-Offs

Published: 23 October 2026, 15:45:00 IST
By Lex Talk Legal Editorial Desk · Original-source news report · Original source ↗

The Congress party has criticised the Centre’s handling of the China border issue after Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP) Director General Shatrujeet Singh Kapur said confrontations with Chinese troops continued at multiple locations along the Line of Actual Control (LAC) in Arunachal Pradesh. In a post on X dated 23 October 2026, Congress cited the 2020 Galwan Valley clash, Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s remarks at an all-party meeting, and an earlier report that Indian security forces had lost access to 26 of 65 patrolling points in eastern Ladakh. The post presents the party’s criticism; its claims about responsibility and current territorial control require careful distinction from official statements and documented events.

What prompted the latest political exchange?

Moneycontrol and The Indian Express reported on 23 October that ITBP chief Shatrujeet Singh Kapur had said face-offs with Chinese troops were continuing at multiple locations along the Arunachal Pradesh border. Kapur reportedly made the remarks at an investiture event ahead of ITBP’s 65th Raising Day. The coverage also noted the force’s preparations, including Shaurya commandos, drone platoons and additional training intended to improve its response to high-altitude border confrontations.

The ITBP chief’s reported comments concern ongoing face-offs in Arunachal Pradesh. They do not, by themselves, establish the status of every disputed location along the LAC or verify each historical claim in Congress’s post.

What did Congress say?

In the post embedded below, Congress quoted AICC Media and Publicity Department chairman Pawan Khera and saluted ITBP personnel, while criticising the government’s handling of China-related security issues. The party alleged that the Prime Minister’s 19 June 2020 remarks gave China a “clean chit” and argued that the earlier loss of access to certain patrolling points had consequences for India’s border position.

Congress’s post refers to several separate issues: the loss of 20 Indian Army personnel in the Galwan clash, the government’s description of the border situation in June 2020, and reporting in 2023 about limited or no Indian patrolling at 26 of 65 patrolling points in eastern Ladakh. These events and claims have different sources and timelines and should not be treated as a single finding.

What did the Prime Minister say in June 2020—and how did the government explain it?

At an all-party meeting on 19 June 2020, held after the Galwan Valley clash, Modi said that no one had entered Indian territory and no Indian post had been captured. Twenty Indian military personnel had died in the clash on 15 June.

The following day, the Prime Minister’s Office issued an official clarification, published by the Press Information Bureau. It described criticism of the remarks as a “mischievous interpretation” and said the Prime Minister’s observations about there being no Chinese presence on India’s side of the LAC referred to the situation after the Indian armed forces had repulsed the attempted transgression. The statement said Chinese forces had sought to erect structures just across the LAC and that Indian soldiers had prevented that attempt.

This official explanation differs from Congress’s description of the remarks as a “clean chit”. This article records both positions rather than treating either political interpretation as a judicial or independent finding about the entire border dispute.

What is the claim about 26 of 65 patrolling points?

The figure cited by Congress traces back to media reporting in January 2023 about a research paper prepared by senior IPS officer P. D. Nitya, then Superintendent of Police in Ladakh, for the annual conference of Directors General and Inspectors General of Police. The paper was reported to say that Indian security forces had lost presence at 26 of 65 patrolling points in eastern Ladakh because of restrictive or no patrolling.

The figure was widely reported at the time and was cited by opposition leaders seeking a discussion in Parliament. It should nevertheless be described precisely: it was a claim reported from a research paper about restrictions on patrolling, not a standalone public order declaring that India had legally ceded sovereignty over 26 locations. The operational meaning of buffer zones, patrol access and territorial claims depends on the specific locations and the understandings reached by the two sides.

What has the government said about patrolling after disengagement?

During a parliamentary exchange in December 2024, External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar responded to questions about reports of inaccessible patrolling points. The Indian Express reported that Jaishankar said he could answer for the government and referred to the disengagement agreements concerning Depsang and Demchok. He said the understanding envisaged Indian security forces going to all patrolling points in Depsang and the eastern limit historically patrolled in that region.

That statement is relevant to the government’s account of the Depsang and Demchok arrangements, but it should not be stretched into a claim that every concern about patrolling throughout the LAC has been resolved. The October 2026 reporting about face-offs in Arunachal Pradesh is a separate development in a different sector.

What can be concluded from the available record?

  • Current reported development: The ITBP chief said face-offs with Chinese troops continued at multiple locations along the Arunachal Pradesh border, according to reports published on 23 October 2026.
  • Congress’s position: The party criticised the Centre’s China policy and revisited the 2020 Galwan remarks and the report about 26 of 65 patrolling points in Ladakh.
  • Official 2020 response: The PMO said Modi’s remarks referred to the situation after Indian forces had repulsed the attempted transgression in Galwan.
  • Historical patrolling-point report: The 26-of-65 figure was attributed to a research paper reported in January 2023; its meaning should not be simplified to a legal finding of territorial surrender.
  • Government’s reported 2024 position: Jaishankar referred to the Depsang and Demchok disengagement understanding and patrolling arrangements in those areas.

Questions about border security and patrol access are matters of public importance. Clear assessment requires location-specific facts, the text of disengagement arrangements, official briefings and current operational information. Political allegations and official explanations should be assessed against that record, without assuming that one statement answers every question about the LAC.

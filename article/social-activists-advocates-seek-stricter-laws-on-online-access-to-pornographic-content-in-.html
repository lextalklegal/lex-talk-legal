Social Activists, Advocates Seek Stricter Laws on Online Access to Pornographic Content in India

Social Activists, Advocates Seek Stricter Laws on Online Access to Pornographic Content in India


New Delhi | October 01,2026

 Anjali Chaudhary 


                        

The debate over online access to pornographic content in India has gained renewed attention, with social activists and legal professionals calling for stronger safeguards and more effective regulation of sexually explicit material available through digital platforms.

The issue involves several interconnected concerns, including the accessibility of age-inappropriate content to children, the circulation of sexually explicit material without consent, and the responsibility of online intermediaries to prevent unlawful content from being hosted or distributed.

India Already Has Laws Regulating Online Obscene and Sexually Explicit Content

India does not have a single law dealing with every aspect of pornography on the internet. However, existing legislation contains provisions dealing with obscene and sexually explicit material in electronic form.

The Information Technology Act, 2000 contains provisions under Sections 67, 67A and 67B relating to the publication or transmission of obscene material, sexually explicit material and material involving children in electronic form. The Government has also previously stated that these provisions can attract criminal penalties.

The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012 (POCSO Act) separately contains provisions concerning the use of children for pornographic purposes and the storage of pornographic material involving children.

Government Tightens Digital-Safety Measures

The Centre has also taken steps aimed at strengthening the obligations of online platforms.

In July 2026, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology said that the Information Technology Act and the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 provide a framework for dealing with unlawful and harmful content online.

The government stated that intermediaries had been reminded of their due-diligence obligations concerning the hosting, publication, transmission, sharing or uploading of vulgar, indecent, obscene, pornographic and other unlawful content.

The government has additionally highlighted measures concerning child sexual abuse material and non-consensual intimate imagery, including a standard operating procedure released in November 2025 for dealing with such content online.

Supreme Court Considered Plea Seeking a National Policy

The broader question of whether India should formulate a national policy prohibiting the viewing of pornographic content has also come before the Supreme Court.

In July 2026, the Supreme Court declined to entertain a PIL seeking directions to the Union Government to formulate a national policy and legal framework to prohibit viewing pornographic content in India.

According to reports of the proceedings, the Court indicated that the issue involved policy and technological considerations and gave the petitioner liberty to approach the competent authorities with a representation.

This development is significant because it distinguishes between the existing criminal-law provisions concerning the creation, publication, transmission or circulation of prohibited material and the separate policy question of regulating an individual's access to adult content.

Child Safety Remains a Major Legal Concern

One of the strongest areas of legal intervention concerns sexually explicit material involving children.

The POCSO Act specifically addresses the use of children for pornographic purposes and related offences.

The Supreme Court has also continued to scrutinise the availability of child sexual exploitation and abuse material on digital platforms. In September 2026, the Court gave the Centre another opportunity to explain the steps taken to implement directions from its 2024 judgment concerning online child sexual abuse material and measures expected from social-media intermediaries.

The issue therefore extends beyond adult pornography and includes the prevention, detection and removal of illegal material involving children.

Why Activists and Advocates Are Calling for Stronger Regulation

Those seeking stricter regulation have raised concerns about the ease with which minors may encounter sexually explicit content online and about the rapid circulation of unlawful material through websites, social-media platforms and other digital services.

Another concern is the distribution of intimate photographs or videos without the consent of the person depicted. Such cases can involve privacy violations, harassment and other criminal offences depending on the circumstances.

The Government has said that its online-safety framework is intended to make the digital environment more open, safe, trusted and accountable, while placing due-diligence responsibilities on intermediaries.

The Legal Debate: Regulation vs. Access

Any proposal for stricter regulation would involve several legal and technological questions.

These include:

  • How should minors' access to age-inappropriate material be prevented?

  • What mechanisms should online platforms use for age assurance?

  • How quickly should unlawful content be removed after receiving a valid complaint or legal direction?

  • What responsibilities should search engines and intermediaries have?

  • How should the law address content shared without the consent of the person depicted?

  • What safeguards should exist against overbroad blocking of lawful online content?

The answers would need to balance online safety, enforcement requirements, privacy and other legal considerations.

What Could Happen Next?

The continuing debate may lead to further representations before the Central Government and relevant ministries, as well as possible litigation concerning specific categories of online content.

For now, India's legal framework already criminalises several forms of obscene, sexually explicit and child-related material in electronic form, while the broader question of prohibiting or substantially restricting access to adult pornography remains a matter of policy and legal debate.

As digital platforms continue to evolve, lawmakers, courts, technology companies and civil-society groups are likely to remain involved in discussions over how online content should be regulated while protecting individuals from unlawful and harmful material.

YouTuber Dhruv Rathee Detained in Gurugram Ahead of CJP’s Jantar Mantar Protest

Published: 10 October 2026, 11:53:43 IST
By Lex Talk Legal Editorial Desk · Educational & informational coverage · Original source ↗


 

New Delhi | October 10, 2026

Popular YouTuber Dhruv Rathee was detained at his residence in Gurugram’s Sector 56 on Saturday, October 10, ahead of a proposed protest organised by the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) at Jantar Mantar in New Delhi. The detention took place amid heightened security arrangements and preventive police action in the national capital ahead of the planned demonstration.

According to The Indian Express, police sources told PTI that Rathee was detained while preparing to join the protest. NDTV also reported the detention, citing police sources. The development has drawn attention to the continuing dispute over permission for public demonstrations and the exercise of constitutional freedoms.

Sources: The Indian Express | NDTV

Why Was Dhruv Rathee Detained?

Reports indicate that Rathee intended to participate in the CJP’s proposed demonstration at Jantar Mantar. The protest was directed against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and concerned allegations of irregularities in the revision of electoral rolls, including the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) process.

On Friday evening, Rathee reportedly announced his intention to participate in the demonstration through a post on X. NDTV further reported that he had returned to India from Germany earlier in the week and had attended a protest in Bengaluru on October 8 relating to the same broader issues.

The allegations concerning electoral-roll revisions remain disputed. Critics have raised concerns about voter-list changes, while the Election Commission and the BJP have denied allegations that the revisions were intended to influence election outcomes.

Sources: NDTV’s report on Rathee’s detention | Reuters’ report on the proposed protest

CJP Condemns the Detention and Demands His Release

The Cockroach Janta Party strongly criticised the detention and called for Rathee’s immediate release. According to reports by The Indian Express and Hindustan Times, the party characterised the action against Rathee and other activists as an attempt to restrict dissent over electoral-roll issues.

These statements represent the party’s allegations and should be distinguished from any independently established finding about the reasons for the detention.

The detention also occurred amid reports that other CJP leaders, activists and protesters had been taken into custody as police attempted to prevent people from reaching the proposed demonstration venue.

Sources: The Indian Express | Hindustan Times

Why Was the Jantar Mantar Protest Restricted?

Delhi Police had denied permission for the proposed demonstration, citing security concerns and procedural requirements. In its reporting, Reuters said police described the wider restrictions as temporary and precautionary measures intended to regulate movement, prevent overcrowding, maintain public order and protect the safety of people in affected areas.

Security arrangements reportedly included barricades, deployment of additional personnel and restrictions affecting access to parts of the capital. Reuters also reported that police had warned that those violating the restrictions would face legal action.

The denial of permission and the subsequent detentions raise separate questions: whether the proposed assembly complied with applicable requirements, what legal grounds were invoked for each detention, and whether the police action followed the procedures prescribed by law.

Source: Reuters — India detains protesters ahead of rally against election chief

Legal Perspective: Freedom of Speech and Peaceful Assembly

The incident brings Articles 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(b) of the Constitution of India into focus.

Article 19(1)(a) protects the right to freedom of speech and expression, while Article 19(1)(b) protects the right to assemble peaceably and without arms. These rights are fundamental to democratic participation, public debate and peaceful political dissent.

However, neither right is absolute. The Constitution permits reasonable restrictions on freedom of speech and expression on specified grounds, including public order, and on peaceful assembly in the interests of public order and the sovereignty and integrity of India.

Authorities may regulate assemblies in accordance with the applicable law. At the same time, police action must have a lawful basis and comply with relevant procedural safeguards.

In Rathee’s case, the news reports reviewed for this article do not establish the precise legal provision invoked for his individual detention, whether a written detention order was issued, or the subsequent outcome of any police proceedings. Those details are important before drawing a definitive conclusion about the legality of the action.

Legal context: Constitution of India — Legislative Department, Government of India

What Happens Next?

The immediate questions concern Rathee’s custody status, the grounds communicated for his detention and whether authorities have issued any further statement. Clarification of these details would help establish the legal circumstances surrounding the incident.

The episode also highlights the continuing tension between public-order management and citizens’ ability to participate in peaceful demonstrations. The legal assessment will ultimately depend on the relevant orders, the factual circumstances and the procedures followed by the authorities.

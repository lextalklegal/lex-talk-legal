Constitutional Boundaries Affirmed: CJI Declines Opposition’s Memorandum on Sub Judice Matters

Published: 12 October 2026, 19:40:09 IST
By Lex Talk Legal Editorial Desk · Educational & informational coverage · Original source ↗



Published: 12 October 2026 | Category: Legal News, Supreme Court of India

Chief Justice of India Surya Kant has turned down a request from the two Leaders of Opposition for a meeting to hand over a memorandum. The request came from Rahul Gandhi (Lok Sabha) and Mallikarjun Kharge (Rajya Sabha), and the letter declining it was reported on 12 October. He cited the separation of powers and the fact that the issues are already before the Court. aninews

What Did the Opposition Ask For?

Kharge and Gandhi wrote on 10 October, seeking a meeting on 12 October between 4 pm and 5 pm. The letter on file lists four topics for the memorandum: metrovaartha

  • The Chief Election Commissioner and the Election Commission of India
  • The alleged arbitrary arrest of several students
  • The closure of metro stations
  • The cancellation of trains, among other issues

Why Did the CJI Decline?

In his reply, the CJI said the issues are already sub judice before the Supreme Court. He said that convening a meeting would breach the constitutional principle of separation of powers. The letter adds that such a meeting would be incompatible with established ethics and judicial propriety, and calls separation of powers an intrinsic part of the basic structure doctrine. verdictum

No Administrative Role, But a Judicial Remedy

The letter also says the Supreme Court has no administrative powers to deal with these issues. It notes that under Article 32, along with its power of judicial review, the Court is duty-bound to consider such matters on the judicial side. The CJI said it would be more appropriate to invoke the Court’s judicial powers than to submit a memorandum on the administrative side. metrovaartha

The letter reaffirms the Court’s commitment to protecting citizens’ Fundamental Rights and says citizens can seek their enforcement at all hours through the procedure known to law.

An Open Door to File a Petition

The letter points to a way forward:

  • Hearing date: Some matters on similar issues are listed for Tuesday, 13 October 2026.
  • Physical filing: A petition can be submitted at the Supreme Court on Monday between 10:30 am and 4:30 pm.
  • E-filing: It can also be filed online, which is open at any time on any day.
  • Assurance: The CJI wrote that any such petition would be heard at the earliest possible opportunity, in accordance with the law.

Related Court Development

A separate Verdictum report says the Supreme Court ordered the removal of Section 163 BNSS notice boards around its premises and asked authorities to file an affidavit explaining the closure of 45 metro stations. This confirms that related issues are already before the Court on the judicial side. verdictum

Why This Matters

  • Separation of powers: The reply shows the judiciary’s position that courts act through judicial proceedings, not administrative meetings.
  • Sub judice caution: Discussing pending cases outside the courtroom can compromise judicial propriety.
  • Constitutional route: Article 32 gives citizens a direct way to approach the Supreme Court for fundamental rights.

FAQs

Why did the CJI decline the meeting?
He said the issues are already sub judice and that a meeting on pending cases would go against the separation of powers and judicial propriety.

Who requested the meeting?
Rahul Gandhi, Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, and Mallikarjun Kharge, Leader of Opposition in the Rajya Sabha.

What did the CJI suggest instead?
Filing a petition under the Court’s judicial jurisdiction, either physically or through e-filing.

What is Article 32?
It is the constitutional provision that lets citizens approach the Supreme Court directly to enforce their Fundamental Rights.

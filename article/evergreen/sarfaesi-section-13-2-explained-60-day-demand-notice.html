SARFAESI Section 13(2) Explained: What Is the 60-Day Demand Notice?

Published: 03 October 2026, 09:10:00 IST
By Lex Talk Legal Editorial Desk · Evergreen legal guide · Primary-source references are included below.

SARFAESI Section 13(2) Explained: What Is the 60-Day Demand Notice?

SARFAESI Section 13(2) is one of the most searched provisions in Indian banking-recovery law. Borrowers often ask: “What is a 13(2) notice?”, “How many days are given?” and “What happens after the notice?” This guide explains the statutory framework in simple language.

Editorial note: This is an educational overview, not legal advice. Statutory provisions, rules, notifications, judicial decisions and facts of individual matters can change or interact differently depending on the case. Readers should verify the current official text and obtain case-specific advice where required.

What is a Section 13(2) notice?

Section 13(2) of the Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 — commonly called the SARFAESI Act — deals with a written demand by a secured creditor. The current official India Code text states that where a secured debt is in default and is classified in the manner contemplated by the Act, the secured creditor may require the borrower by written notice to discharge the liabilities in full within 60 days from the date of the notice. If the liability is not discharged, the secured creditor may exercise the rights referred to in section 13(4), subject to the Act and applicable rules.

Why is the 60-day period important?

The 60-day period is the statutory demand period stated in section 13(2). It gives the borrower a defined period after receipt of the demand notice to address the claimed liability, make the required payment or take appropriate legal and commercial steps.

The notice is therefore not the same as immediate physical possession of the property. Section 13(2) is the demand stage. The later enforcement measures are dealt with separately under section 13(4).

What should a borrower check after receiving a 13(2) notice?

A borrower should read the notice carefully and compare it with the underlying loan and security documents. Questions can include:

  • What loan account or facility is referred to?
  • What amount is demanded and how is it stated?
  • When was the account classified as a non-performing asset, where applicable?
  • What secured asset is involved?
  • Is the security description accurate?
  • Are there factual or calculation errors?
  • Are there contractual or statutory issues that need timely attention?

Whether any objection has legal merit is fact-specific and should be assessed against the complete record rather than the notice alone.

Can a borrower send a representation or objection?

Yes. Section 13(3A) deals with the borrower's representation or objection to the demand notice. The secured creditor is required to consider such representation or objection. Where it does not accept the representation or objection, the creditor must communicate the reasons for non-acceptance within the statutory framework.

Importantly, section 13(3A) does not itself create a right to approach the DRT merely because the borrower disagrees with the reasons communicated at the 13(2) stage. Section 17 of SARFAESI is connected to measures referred to in section 13(4).

What happens if the 60 days expire?

If the liabilities are not discharged within the statutory notice period, section 13(4) permits specified enforcement measures in the circumstances covered by the Act. These may include taking possession of the secured assets, taking over management in the situations specified by the Act, appointing a manager, or requiring certain persons who have acquired the secured asset from the borrower not to deal with it, depending on the statutory conditions.

Therefore, the end of the 60-day period can mark a transition from the demand stage to enforcement measures. It does not mean that every case automatically follows the exact same sequence; the creditor's action must still comply with the SARFAESI Act, applicable rules and the facts of the secured transaction.

Does a 13(2) notice mean the property is already possessed?

No. A 13(2) notice is a demand notice. Possession is a different enforcement stage. The statutory scheme distinguishes the demand under section 13(2) from measures under section 13(4) and the possession assistance mechanism under section 14.

What is the difference between Section 13(2) and Section 13(4)?

ProvisionBasic function
Section 13(2)Written demand requiring discharge of the secured liability within 60 days.
Section 13(3A)Consideration of the borrower's representation or objection.
Section 13(4)Specified enforcement measures after the statutory demand stage, subject to the Act.
Section 17Remedy before DRT against measures referred to in section 13(4).
Section 14Assistance of Chief Metropolitan Magistrate or District Magistrate for taking possession in the circumstances covered by the Act.

What should a borrower do after receiving the notice?

The first step is usually to establish the factual position: obtain the complete loan statement, security documents, prior correspondence, demand notice and relevant payment records. If the borrower disputes the amount or enforcement basis, the objection should be considered within the statutory timeline and with reference to the entire record.

If enforcement measures are subsequently taken under section 13(4), a person aggrieved by those measures may have the statutory remedy under section 17 before the DRT, subject to the limitation and jurisdiction provisions.

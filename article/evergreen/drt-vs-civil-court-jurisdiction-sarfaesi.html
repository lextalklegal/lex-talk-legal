DRT vs Civil Court in India: Jurisdiction, SARFAESI and Which Forum Deals With What?

Published: 03 October 2026, 09:40:00 IST
By Lex Talk Legal Editorial Desk · Evergreen legal guide · Primary-source references are included below.

DRT vs Civil Court in India: Jurisdiction, SARFAESI and Which Forum Deals With What?

“Should I go to the civil court or DRT?” is a common question in banking and recovery disputes. The correct answer depends on the legal route, cause of action, relief sought and statute involved. This guide explains the framework without reducing every banking dispute to a single forum.

Editorial note: This is an educational overview, not legal advice. Statutory provisions, rules, notifications, judicial decisions and facts of individual matters can change or interact differently depending on the case. What is the basic role of DRT?

DRTs are statutory tribunals established under the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993. The RDB Act provides a framework for applications by banks and financial institutions for recovery of debts and for appeals to the Appellate Tribunal.

What does a civil court normally do?

Civil courts exercise jurisdiction over civil disputes subject to the limits created by legislation. In banking matters, however, several specialised statutes create separate mechanisms or bars of jurisdiction. Therefore, a person should not assume that the mere existence of a civil dispute automatically determines the forum.

What does SARFAESI Section 34 say?

Section 34 of the SARFAESI Act provides that civil courts shall not have jurisdiction to entertain a suit or proceeding in respect of a matter which a DRT or Appellate Tribunal is empowered by or under SARFAESI to determine. The section also contains a bar on injunctions in relation to action taken or to be taken under powers conferred by SARFAESI.

This statutory text is important, but applying a jurisdictional bar in an actual case requires analysing the precise relief and cause of action. The existence of SARFAESI does not mean that every conceivable dispute concerning a bank and borrower belongs in the DRT.

DRT under RDB Act versus DRT under SARFAESI

These are related but distinct statutory routes.

RouteCommon purpose
RDB Act — Section 19Bank/financial institution recovery application before DRT.
SARFAESI — Section 17Challenge by a person aggrieved by a measure referred to in Section 13(4).
SARFAESI — Section 18Appeal to Appellate Tribunal against a DRT order under Section 17, subject to the statutory framework.

Can a borrower file in civil court to stop SARFAESI?

Section 34 creates a civil-court jurisdiction bar in respect of matters the DRT or Appellate Tribunal is empowered to determine under SARFAESI, and it also addresses injunctions against actions under the Act. Whether a particular suit falls within that bar is a legal question based on the pleadings and the nature of the relief.

For this reason, forum selection should be assessed before filing rather than after proceedings have already begun.

What if the dispute involves title or a person who is not the borrower?

Third-party property disputes can require more detailed analysis. The question may involve the nature of the claimed right, when it arose, the security interest, the relief requested and the statutory jurisdiction available to the DRT. SARFAESI section 17 also expressly refers to “any person” including the borrower in relation to specified measures.

Can a civil court grant an injunction against a SARFAESI action?

Section 34 expressly contains an injunction-related bar for action taken or to be taken under powers conferred by SARFAESI, subject to the scope of the statute. A litigant should therefore not assume that filing a civil suit automatically produces an injunction against bank enforcement.

Practical forum-selection checklist

  • Identify exactly what the bank has done: demand, possession, auction, sale, recovery application or another action.
  • Identify the statute invoked by the bank.
  • Identify the relief you actually need.
  • Check the relevant limitation period.
  • Check statutory jurisdiction before filing.
  • Review any existing DRT, DRAT, High Court or civil-court proceedings.
  • Obtain case-specific legal advice where jurisdiction is disputed.

Bottom line

DRT vs Civil Court is not a simple “choose DRT for all bank cases” question. The forum is driven by the statute and the nature of the dispute. In SARFAESI matters, section 17 provides a specific DRT remedy for measures referred to in section 13(4), while section 34 restricts civil-court jurisdiction over matters entrusted to the DRT/DRAT.

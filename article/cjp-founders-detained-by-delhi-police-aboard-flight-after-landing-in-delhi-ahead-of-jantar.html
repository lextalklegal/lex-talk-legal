CJP Founders Detained by Delhi Police Aboard Flight After Landing in Delhi Ahead of Jantar Mantar Protest

Published: 10 October 2026, 11:48:35 IST
By Lex Talk Legal Editorial Desk · Educational & informational coverage · Original source ↗

 



New Delhi | October 10, 2026

The founders and leaders of the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) were detained by Delhi Police shortly after their Mumbai–Delhi flight landed on Saturday, ahead of the party’s proposed demonstration at Jantar Mantar. The incident has drawn attention amid heightened security arrangements in the national capital and a dispute over the proposed protest.

According to reports by The Indian Express and Reuters, CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke, along with party leaders Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka, was detained after the aircraft arrived in Delhi. The party alleged that the action was intended to prevent its leaders from reaching the protest venue.

Why Was CJP Planning a Protest in Delhi?

The Cockroach Janta Party had called for a demonstration at Jantar Mantar targeting Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. The proposed protest concerned allegations of voter-list manipulation during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls and issues relating to Form 6, which is used for voter registration.

These are allegations raised by the protesters and should not be treated as established findings.

CJP demanded the immediate and unconditional release of its detained leaders, according to its statement reported by The Indian Express.

Delhi Police Had Denied Permission for the Demonstration

The detentions came after authorities refused permission for the proposed gathering. In a report published on October 9, The Indian Express said the Ministry of Home Affairs had raised concerns about possible law-and-order implications and referred to procedural non-compliance and Supreme Court directions governing assemblies in the Jantar Mantar area.

Authorities said preventive measures were necessary to maintain public order and protect critical infrastructure. The wider security arrangements reportedly included coordination between Delhi Police and authorities in other states and Union Territories.

The stated security rationale and the party’s allegations about the purpose of the detentions are distinct issues; the available reporting does not establish that a court has adjudicated the legality of the individual detentions.

Other CJP-Linked Detentions Reported

The Indian Express also reported that YouTuber Dhruv Rathee and Ratna Singh, identified as the head of CJP’s legal team, were detained at their residences earlier in the day.

The reported actions occurred as police sought to prevent protesters from reaching the proposed demonstration site. Further details concerning the legal grounds, duration and subsequent status of each detention were not established in the reports cited here.

What Does the Incident Raise From a Legal Perspective?

The episode raises questions about the balance between public-order restrictions and the exercise of constitutional freedoms.

Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution of India protects freedom of speech and expression, while Article 19(1)(b) protects the right to assemble peaceably and without arms. These rights are subject to the reasonable restrictions specified in the Constitution, including restrictions connected with public order.

The existence of a constitutional right to peaceful assembly does not automatically entitle an organiser to hold a gathering at any venue without complying with applicable legal requirements. Equally, restrictions and preventive police action must have a lawful basis and remain subject to applicable safeguards and judicial scrutiny.

The legality of any particular detention would depend on the relevant facts, the legal authority invoked, the grounds communicated and the procedures followed. The available news reports do not provide enough information to reach a definitive legal conclusion about the individual detentions.

A Developing Story

The detention of the CJP leaders marks a significant development ahead of the party’s proposed Jantar Mantar demonstration. The competing concerns—public order, compliance with restrictions on assemblies, and the protection of constitutional freedoms—are likely to remain central to discussion of the incident.

Bhagwant Mann Questions Amit Shah Over Drugs, Criticises Congress in Punjab Assembly

Published: 23 October 2026, 13:15:00 IST
By Lex Talk Legal Editorial Desk · Original-source news report · Original source ↗

A post shared by AAP Punjab on X on 23 October 2026 says Chief Minister Bhagwant Mann addressed the media from the Punjab Vidhan Sabha, questioning Union Home Minister Amit Shah over the drug menace and alleged trafficking through Gujarat ports. The party’s summary also says Mann criticised Congress over disruption of Assembly proceedings and explained his account of the recent Delhi airport episode. The claims and explanations below are attributed to the relevant speakers and reports; the post itself is not independent verification.

What does the AAP Punjab post say?

The party’s post summarises three points from Mann’s media address: questions directed at Amit Shah about narcotics and Gujarat ports; criticism of Congress over disruption of Assembly proceedings; and an explanation of the difficulties Mann reportedly faced at Delhi airport. The post includes a video, which is embedded below so readers can view the source material in its original context.

Mann’s questions to Amit Shah over drugs

The drug issue has become a point of political confrontation in Punjab. In remarks reported on 20 October, Mann challenged the Centre and Amit Shah over the interception of narcotics, border security and the alleged movement of drugs through Gujarat ports. The New Indian Express reported Mann’s criticism of the Centre’s response and his reference to consignments seized at Kandla Port. These were political claims made by Mann; the statement alone does not establish the full origin, route or responsibility for any particular narcotics consignment.

The Indian Express has also reported on the exchange between Shah and the Punjab leadership over drug-related violence, policing and responsibility for tackling the state’s drug problem. As with any political dispute, accusations by one side should be distinguished from independently established findings and official investigative records.

Why was the Assembly session contentious?

The Punjab Vidhan Sabha’s six-day session began on 22 October. The Times of India reported that the first day’s proceedings ended after roughly nine minutes following obituary references, with the Speaker saying the House would meet again the next day. Congress leaders accused the AAP government of avoiding debate on drugs and law and order; the Speaker defended the decision by referring to Assembly tradition and the significance of paying tribute to victims.

AAP Punjab’s 23 October post says Mann criticised Congress for disrupting proceedings. This report attributes that criticism to the party’s post rather than treating either side’s political characterisation as a neutral finding. The published reports describe competing explanations for how the session was handled.

What is known about the Delhi airport episode?

The AAP post also says Mann explained the “real reason” for the trouble at Delhi airport. Earlier reports by The Tribune and The Indian Express said AAP alleged that Mann was kept at the airport for around three hours after returning from Paris and later received a ₹50,000 challan. AAP representatives linked the episode to pressure from above, but the exact grounds for the challan and the circumstances of the delay were not independently established in the cited reporting.

Because the supplied X caption is a summary rather than a full transcript of Mann’s explanation, readers should watch the embedded video for his exact words. Any definitive conclusion about the airport incident would require the relevant official documentation and responses from the authorities involved.

Key distinctions for readers

  • What the post confirms: AAP Punjab published a video post on 23 October and described Mann’s media address as covering drugs, Assembly proceedings and the airport episode.
  • What is reported elsewhere: News outlets have documented the ongoing political dispute over drug trafficking and the competing accounts surrounding the Assembly session and airport incident.
  • What remains a claim: Political allegations about responsibility for drug trafficking, intentional disruption or the motivation behind airport action should not be treated as proven solely because a party or public figure has stated them.
  • Where to verify: Readers can consult the Punjab Vidhan Sabha’s official proceedings and notices, and any publicly available official records relevant to a specific drug seizure or airport challan.

Saurabh Bharadwaj Alleges DJB Contractor Set Up Before Yamuna Defoaming Tender’s Financial Bid

Published: 23 October 2026, 13:30:00 IST
By Lex Talk Legal Editorial Desk · Original-source news report · Original source ↗

AAP Delhi leader Saurabh Bharadwaj has questioned the Delhi Jal Board’s tender process for removing froth from the Yamuna near Kalindi Kunj, alleging that equipment and workers associated with Icon Instruments were at the site before the tender’s financial bid had been opened. The allegation was repeated in a post on X on 22 October 2026. Published reporting also records a government official’s explanation that preparations for the anti-froth drive had begun and that separate bids were invited for different components. The available information raises questions about timing and authorisation, but does not by itself establish that the procurement process was manipulated.

What did Saurabh Bharadwaj allege?

In his X post, Bharadwaj wrote that an employee of Icon Instruments had said the Delhi Jal Board had instructed the company to begin work before the tender was opened. In his earlier public remarks, he said that equipment, chemical containers, pumps and labour were already present at the Kalindi Kunj site. He questioned how the company could have deployed the setup while the financial bid was, according to him, still pending.

These are allegations made by a political leader and should be read as such. Whether the site setup was authorised preparatory activity, whether any work had formally commenced, and who issued any instruction would need to be established through the relevant tender papers, work orders, site records and responses from the department and contractor.

What has been reported independently?

Hindustan Times reported on 22 October that Bharadwaj said the financial bid for the anti-froth work had not yet been opened, despite a contractor having set up tents and equipment at the site. The report also quoted a senior government official saying preparations for the anti-froth drive ahead of Chhath Puja had begun, that multiple agencies were being engaged for procuring chemicals, and that separate bids had been invited for the sprinkling unit. The same report said the Delhi Jal Board spokesperson did not respond to the allegations by the time it was published.

The Tribune also reported AAP’s allegation that equipment for the de-foaming work was present at Kalindi Kunj before the tender was opened and that the party demanded an inquiry. These reports confirm that the allegation and the competing explanation were publicly reported; they do not independently establish that a tender was improperly awarded.

What do the public tender listings show?

The Government of NCT of Delhi’s e-procurement portal listed several related Delhi Jal Board requirements for the 2026 anti-froth operation, including:

  • Short Press NIT No. 07 (26-27)/CWA (W&S)-I, Item 01: supply of defoamer concentrate for the sprinkler system at Okhla Barrage, Kalindi Kunj, during Chhath Puja 2026. The searchable listing showed publication on 16 October 2026 and a bid-opening date of 21 October 2026 at 3:30 PM.
  • Short Press NIT No. 06 (26-27)/CWA (W&S)-I, Item 01: a separate requirement relating to installation and commissioning of a chemical preparation plant and operation and maintenance of preparation/sprinkling. The listing showed publication on 13 October 2026 and a bid-opening date of 19 October 2026 at 3:30 PM.

The presence of separate tender listings is consistent with the official’s reported explanation that different components were being procured through separate bids. However, the portal’s listed bid-opening dates alone do not answer whether the financial bid relevant to the equipment seen at Kalindi Kunj had been opened at the time in question, what site activity was permitted before award, or whether Icon Instruments had received a valid written instruction. Those questions require the specific tender documents, bid-stage records and any work order or written site direction.

Readers can check the Government of NCT of Delhi e-Procurement portal by searching the relevant Delhi Jal Board notice numbers and work descriptions. The tender documents, rather than social-media claims alone, are the primary records for verifying procurement stages and conditions.

Why the timing matters

Public procurement typically involves distinct steps such as tender publication, bid submission, technical evaluation, financial-bid opening, award and issuance of a work order. The precise sequence and permitted preparatory activity depend on the tender conditions and the instructions issued by the procuring authority. Equipment being visible at a site before one of those stages may raise a legitimate question, but it does not, without more evidence, establish that a contractor was selected in advance or that a law was violated.

To resolve the dispute, the key records would include the tender and corrigenda, bid-opening minutes, evaluation/award decision, any written work order or site instruction, and the identity and authority of the official who issued it. A response from the Delhi Jal Board, the responsible minister and Icon Instruments would also help clarify the timeline.

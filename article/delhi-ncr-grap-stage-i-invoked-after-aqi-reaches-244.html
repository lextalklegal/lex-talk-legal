Delhi-NCR Invokes GRAP Stage-I After AQI Reaches 244; Measures Explained

Published: 21 October 2026, 00:00:00 IST
By Lex Talk Legal Editorial Desk · Original-source news report · Original source ↗

Delhi-NCR has moved to Stage-I of the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) after Delhi’s Air Quality Index (AQI) was reported at 244, in the “Poor” category, at 2 PM on 21 October 2026. DD News said the CAQM GRAP Sub-Committee reviewed the change and referred to IMD/IITM forecasts indicating that air quality could remain in the Poor category over the coming days.

What was reported on 21 October?

In its post dated 21 October, DD News said Delhi’s AQI had risen from 179 (“Moderate”) on 20 October to 244 (“Poor”) at 2 PM on 21 October. The post attributed the Stage-I implementation to a review by the Sub-Committee on the Graded Response Action Plan under the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM).

Reading note: The figure of 244 is described in the source as a 2 PM reading. It should not automatically be treated as the final daily average. AQI readings can change during the day; readers should check current official updates for the latest position.

What does GRAP Stage-I mean?

CAQM identifies Stage-I with the “Poor” AQI band of 201–300. It is the first stage of the response schedule and calls for agencies to strengthen routine pollution-control and dust-mitigation measures. The exact directions in force should be read with the latest CAQM order and instructions issued by the relevant authorities.

What measures are generally covered?

CAQM’s published Stage-I summary lists measures such as mechanised road sweeping and water sprinkling, regular collection and removal of municipal solid waste and construction-and-demolition (C&D) waste, prevention of open waste burning, and dust-mitigation measures at C&D sites.

These are implementation measures for the responsible agencies and local authorities. The announcement does not, by itself, establish that every measure is being enforced identically at every location; residents and businesses should follow applicable official orders and local instructions.

What should readers check next?

For a current AQI reading, use the official CAQM website and the relevant air-quality dashboard. For the background to the Stage-I response schedule, see CAQM’s official material and its 16 April 2026 Stage-I press release. That earlier release describes the standard action framework; it is not presented here as the 21 October order.

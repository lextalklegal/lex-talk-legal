Delhi-NCR GRAP Stage-I Explained: AQI 244, Pollution-Control Measures & What Residents Should Know

Published: 21 October 2026, 00:00:00 IST ·Updated: 21 October 2026, 17:10:00 IST
By Lex Talk Legal Editorial Desk · Original-source news report · Original source ↗

On 21 October 2026, DD News reported that Delhi’s Air Quality Index (AQI) had risen from 179 (“Moderate”) on 20 October to 244 (“Poor”) at 2 PM on 21 October. The post said the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) GRAP Sub-Committee had reviewed the deterioration and that measures under Stage-I of the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) were being implemented across Delhi-NCR. Other same-day reports also described Stage-I as activated. This guide explains what that means in everyday language, who is expected to act, and what residents should check before assuming a particular restriction applies to them.

Quick takeaway: An AQI of 244 falls in the “Poor” band (201–300), which corresponds to GRAP Stage-I. The core Stage-I response focuses on dust, waste and construction-site controls, alongside enforcement of the applicable schedule. It is not, by itself, proof that every school, office or private vehicle is subject to a blanket shutdown or ban. The exact current order and any separate local directions matter.

1. What exactly happened?

The source post gave two readings: 179 on 20 October (“Moderate”) and 244 at 2 PM on 21 October (“Poor”). That is a reported rise of 65 AQI points between the cited readings. Treat 244 as the time-stamped reading stated in the post—not automatically as the final daily average, a reading from every NCR city, or a measure of the amount of one particular pollutant. AQI can vary by time and monitoring location.

The DD News post attributed the Stage-I response to a review by CAQM’s GRAP Sub-Committee. The Indian Express, The Economic Times and The Times of India also reported the Stage-I implementation on 21 October. The published details of the current GRAP schedule are available on the official CAQM website, which lists the September 2026 revised GRAP schedule and Direction No. 104 dated 29 September 2026.

Source distinction: The AQI figures and the reported 21 October activation above are taken from DD News and same-day reporting. The explanation of the standing Stage-I measures below is based on CAQM’s published GRAP overview and schedule. Readers should use the latest CAQM order for the exact operative wording, additional directions and any later changes.

2. AQI in simple language: what does 244 mean?

AQI stands for Air Quality Index. Think of it as a public-facing scale that helps people understand the overall air-quality category. It is an index—not a percentage and not the concentration of a single pollutant. India’s AQI system uses pollutant data to communicate categories and possible health impacts.

AQI rangeCategoryPlain-language meaning
0–50GoodLowest concern on this scale.
51–100SatisfactoryGenerally acceptable; sensitive people may notice discomfort.
101–200ModeratePeople with asthma or heart/lung conditions, children and older adults may be more affected.
201–300PoorProlonged exposure may cause breathing discomfort; extra care is sensible for vulnerable groups.
301–400Very PoorGreater concern; respiratory effects can become more likely with prolonged exposure.
401–500SevereHighest band on the national AQI scale; even healthy people may experience effects.

The AQI category names above follow the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) framework. CAQM’s GRAP schedule uses corresponding action stages: Stage-I for Poor (201–300), Stage-II for Very Poor (301–400), Stage-III for 401–450, and Stage-IV above 450. That is why a GRAP stage and the name of an AQI category should not be treated as interchangeable labels in every context: the action schedule divides the upper ranges into its own stages.

For this incident: 244 is within the Poor band and therefore aligns with Stage-I. It is not in the Very Poor band and the cited number does not, by itself, establish the AQI for every neighbourhood or city in NCR.

3. What is GRAP, and why does it have stages?

GRAP means Graded Response Action Plan. It is a pre-defined response framework used in Delhi-NCR when air quality deteriorates. In simple terms, it is a ladder of actions: as air quality reaches more concerning bands, the schedule provides additional measures. CAQM publishes the schedule and issues directions; implementing agencies are expected to carry out the actions assigned to them.

The approach is intended to move action beyond general advice. For example, road-dust suppression, timely waste collection and dust controls at construction sites can be assigned to the agencies and operators best placed to implement them. The schedule also allows the authorities to consider forecasts and issue operational orders. Therefore, people should check the latest CAQM notification rather than infer every restriction from one AQI number alone.

4. What does Stage-I actually focus on?

CAQM’s public overview identifies three central Stage-I areas. The detailed schedule gives implementing responsibilities to municipal bodies, road-owning/maintaining agencies, regulators and construction stakeholders. In plain language, the work is as follows:

A. Road dust: sweep properly, control dust, dispose of it safely

  • Carry out periodic mechanised road sweeping and/or water sprinkling where directed and appropriate.
  • Collect the dust rather than merely shifting it to another roadside location, and send it to designated disposal sites.
  • Focus on dusty stretches, road shoulders and locations where loose material can be lifted into the air.

Why this matters: Traffic and wind can lift settled dust back into the air. Sweeping without proper collection and disposal can simply move the problem around.

B. Waste: remove it on time and do not burn it in the open

  • Ensure regular lifting of municipal solid waste (MSW), construction-and-demolition (C&D) waste and other listed waste streams from designated collection/dump sites.
  • Prevent illegal dumping on open land and prevent open burning of waste.
  • Report persistent waste accumulation or visible burning to the responsible municipal/local authority using its official complaint channel.

Why this matters: An unmanaged waste pile is not just an eyesore. Burning waste releases smoke and pollutants close to the people who live or work nearby.

C. Construction and demolition: keep dust and debris contained

  • Follow the applicable dust-mitigation and environmental-management requirements at construction and demolition sites.
  • Keep construction materials and debris contained and covered as required; do not allow loose material to spill or be blown into public areas.
  • Use the required waste collection, transport and processing/recycling arrangements. Follow registration and remote-monitoring requirements where the applicable CAQM directions require them.
  • Owners, contractors and site managers should check that the actual site practices match the current directions—not just that a noticeboard or plan exists.

Who implements these actions? CAQM sets/coordinates the GRAP framework and issues directions. Local urban bodies and other designated public agencies handle road cleaning and waste operations; road-owning agencies manage their stretches; regulators inspect compliance; and construction stakeholders must meet the requirements that apply to their sites. The detailed schedule names the relevant implementing agencies for each action.

5. What should an ordinary resident do today?

GRAP is not only a government checklist. Residents can help avoid adding smoke and dust, use official information, and report observable problems. These are practical steps—not a substitute for any specific order or medical advice.

  • Check the current AQI, not just yesterday’s headline. Look at the time and location of the reading on CPCB/CAQM sources; do not assume the entire NCR has the same number.
  • Avoid open burning. Do not burn leaves, household waste or other material outdoors. Ask the local body for the proper collection route.
  • Reduce unnecessary vehicle idling. Keep vehicles maintained, keep required pollution-under-control documentation valid, and consider public transport or car-pooling where practical.
  • Notice dust and debris near construction sites. If dust is visibly escaping or C&D waste is being dumped illegally, document the time and location safely and use the relevant official complaint channel. Do not confront workers or enter a hazardous site.
  • Take prolonged exposure seriously. CPCB notes that Poor-category air can cause breathing discomfort on prolonged exposure. Children, older adults and people with heart or lung disease may need extra care; follow personal medical advice and seek appropriate help for significant or worsening symptoms.
  • Follow official local instructions. Conditions and additional rules can differ by area and can change when CAQM or a local authority issues a new direction.

6. What should businesses, RWAs and construction operators do?

Construction and demolition sites: review the current CAQM schedule and site-specific directions; make sure dust-control equipment and practices are operational; contain material and debris; keep the required records/portal registration up to date where applicable; and arrange lawful C&D waste handling.

Market associations and resident welfare associations (RWAs): make sure common areas do not become waste-burning or debris-dumping points; communicate collection arrangements; and direct complaints to the official local channel. Do not create your own restrictions unless there is a lawful basis or official instruction.

Municipal and road agencies: Stage-I is not satisfied by an announcement alone. The practical test is visible implementation: timely collection, mechanised sweeping, dust suppression, safe disposal and inspection where prescribed.

Commercial establishments: check the latest operative CAQM and local orders for any sector-specific fuel, generator or operating conditions before relying on forwarded messages or social-media summaries. This explainer does not add a separate restriction that is not clearly supported by the cited schedule.

7. Does Stage-I mean schools will close or private cars will be banned?

Not automatically from the Stage-I label alone. Stage-I is the first GRAP action band. It should not be presented as a blanket order closing every school or office, banning every private car, or bringing in an odd-even scheme unless the relevant government/CAQM order actually says so. Higher GRAP stages and separate statutory or local directions can contain additional measures. Read the latest notice and check whether a specific measure applies to your category, area and date.

This distinction matters because incorrect forwards can cause confusion: one measure may apply only to particular vehicles, certain operators, defined locations, or a higher stage. Verify the precise wording, effective date, exemptions and issuing authority before acting on a claimed ban.

8. What could happen next?

If pollution remains poor, it may continue to require Stage-I measures and monitoring. If AQI forecasts indicate a more serious or sustained deterioration, CAQM may consider action under a higher stage in accordance with the revised schedule and issue further directions. That is a conditional framework—not a prediction that schools, offices or traffic will definitely face additional restrictions.

The practical sequence is: check the latest AQI and forecast; read the current CAQM order; identify the stage and measures actually invoked; then follow the instructions relevant to your area or activity. Do not assume that Stage-II or Stage-III rules have become active simply because the headline mentions GRAP.

9. The legal and administrative angle

The Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) is the central coordinating body for air-quality management in the National Capital Region and adjoining areas. Its published GRAP schedule allocates actions and identifies implementing agencies. This creates an administrative framework for coordinated action; it does not mean every item is performed by one authority or that a news report replaces the underlying order.

For a legal/compliance question—particularly about a construction site, commercial operation, generator, vehicle class or any alleged violation—the relevant documents are the latest CAQM schedule/order, any separate direction applicable to the activity, and the notice or instruction issued by the competent implementing authority. Preserve the notice and check its date, legal basis, addressee, scope and any stated exemption. This article is general public information, not a site-specific legal opinion.

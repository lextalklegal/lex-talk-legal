Amit Shah Targets Rahul Gandhi Over ‘Vote Chori’ Claims, Alleges Bid to Protect Infiltrators

Published: 23 October 2026, 13:45:00 IST
By Lex Talk Legal Editorial Desk · Original-source news report · Original source ↗

Union Home Minister Amit Shah criticised Congress leader Rahul Gandhi in a post on X dated 22 October 2026, alleging that Gandhi’s “vote chori” campaign was intended to protect the votes of people Shah described as “infiltrators”. Shah also linked opposition frustration to the BJP forming governments in multiple states. The statement comes amid continuing political disagreement over electoral-roll integrity and the Special Intensive Revision (SIR). Shah’s allegation is a political claim, not an independent finding about the motives behind Congress’s objections.

What did Amit Shah say?

In the post embedded below, Shah said that the BJP’s victories in successive states had left Rahul Gandhi frustrated and alleged that the opposition’s “vote chori” campaign was aimed at protecting infiltrators. The post states Shah’s position but does not present evidence establishing Gandhi’s motive or the status of any individual voter.

The Times of India reported on 22 October that Shah made similar remarks at a Delhi event, accusing Gandhi of using allegations of electoral irregularities to explain Congress’s electoral defeats. Shah also argued that opposition parties question the Election Commission when they lose but accept electoral outcomes when their side wins.

Why is the “vote chori” dispute continuing?

Rahul Gandhi and other opposition leaders have accused the BJP and the Election Commission of India (ECI) of electoral irregularities, including concerns linked to voter-roll revision. The opposition has demanded accountability over the process and has raised objections to the manner in which some names may be added to or removed from electoral rolls. These accusations are contested and need to be assessed against the underlying documents, statutory procedures and official records.

In September 2026, The Indian Express reported on objections recorded by two Election Commissioners regarding certain decisions concerning the SIR and the way those decisions were handled internally. The report became part of the political argument over how the voter-roll revision was being conducted. It is relevant context to the public dispute, but it does not by itself establish every claim made by either political side about the election results.

What has the Election Commission said?

In coverage published on 23 September 2026, Moneycontrol reported that the Election Commission said decisions relating to the SIR had the approval of all three Election Commissioners. The report described this as the Commission’s response amid renewed allegations from Gandhi. That position differs from the opposition’s criticism of the process.

Readers should distinguish an institution’s stated procedure from the separate question of whether a particular voter-roll entry, deletion or local administrative decision complied with the applicable rules. Such questions are best tested using official notices, voter-roll records, objections and appeals, and any relevant orders or findings—not by treating a political accusation or denial as conclusive on its own.

What does SIR mean?

SIR stands for Special Intensive Revision, an exercise undertaken to review and update electoral rolls under the Election Commission’s framework. Electoral-roll processes can include verification, publication of draft lists, claims and objections, and final publication according to the applicable instructions and schedule. Citizens who have questions about their own registration should consult the official ECI portal or their State Chief Electoral Officer for the correct local procedure and deadlines.

Questions about whether a person is eligible to be registered, whether a name has been incorrectly included or omitted, or whether an official followed the prescribed process should be resolved through the legally available procedures and evidence. The term “infiltrator” in Shah’s post is his characterisation and should not be taken as a verified description of any specific voter on the basis of this post alone.

Claims, evidence and accountability

  • Shah’s stated position: He says “vote chori” allegations are politically motivated and intended to protect votes of people he describes as infiltrators.
  • Opposition’s stated position: Rahul Gandhi and other opposition leaders have alleged irregularities and raised questions about the conduct and oversight of electoral-roll revision.
  • ECI position reported in September: Moneycontrol reported that the Commission said SIR decisions had approval from all three Election Commissioners.
  • What would help clarify disputed cases: The relevant rolls, revision instructions, objections and appeal records, official replies, and any judicial or administrative findings concerning a specific claim.
